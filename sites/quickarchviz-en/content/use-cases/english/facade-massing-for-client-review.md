Architectural teams often need a convincing visual direction long before the final rendering package is ready. At that stage, the goal is not photorealistic perfection. The goal is a shared picture of the design intent, enough for the client to react to massing, mood, facade rhythm, and context.

QuickArchViz helps bridge that gap. Instead of waiting until every material and detail is modeled, an architect can upload a clear early view and describe the intended atmosphere. The output becomes a discussion artifact: something easier to understand than a raw model screenshot, but still fast enough for an active design process.

## When to use this workflow

Use it when the client needs to see direction, not final documentation. It is especially useful before a concept presentation, design option review, or internal checkpoint where the team needs alignment on the visual character of a proposal.

## What the architect should prepare

- A simple exported view with the intended camera angle.
- A short description of material intent, mood, and context.
- A list of elements that must not change, such as proportions, openings, or massing.
- A clear goal for the image: client preview, internal critique, or portfolio draft.

## How it helps the conversation

The before-after comparison makes the value easy to explain. The client can still see the original architectural structure, but the improved version carries more atmosphere, scale, and presentation quality. That makes feedback more concrete and keeps the design discussion moving.
